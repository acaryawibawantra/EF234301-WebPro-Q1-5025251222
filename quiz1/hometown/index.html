Hometown

Denpasar, Bali

Denpasar is the capital of Bali and the city where I grew up. It is the island's busiest city, but temples, traditional markets, and rice-field edges still sit right next to offices, schools, and malls.

Culture

Most people in Denpasar follow Balinese Hinduism, and it shows in everyday life. Small woven offerings (canang sari) are placed on sidewalks and shop fronts each morning, and the sound of gamelan from a temple ceremony is a normal part of the week. Big celebrations like Galungan decorate the streets with penjor, tall bamboo poles lined along the roads. Family temples, community groups called banjar, and traditional ceremonies are still woven into city life.

Everyday life

A day in Denpasar starts early. Traders set up at Pasar Badung, the island's biggest traditional market, long before office hours. During the day the streets fill with motorbikes, and people eat at simple family food stalls called warungs. The weather is warm all year, so life happens outside: in the late afternoon people jog or relax around Puputan Square in Renon, and on weekends many families head to Sanur beach to watch the sunrise or swim in calm water.

Interesting facts

  • The name: "Denpasar" comes from den pasar, which means "north of the market".
  • Capital city: it has been the capital of Bali since 1958, when Bali became a province.
  • Population: more than 700,000 residents, making it the most populous city on the island.
  • Sanur: one of Bali's best-known beaches is part of the city, on its east coast.
  • Landmarks: the Bajra Sandhi Monument, Bali Museum, and Jagatnatha Temple are all in Denpasar.
